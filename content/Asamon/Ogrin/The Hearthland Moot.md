## Overview

_The Hearthland Moot is a vast confederation of independent halfling settlements spread across the fertile plains, forests, rivers and foothills of central Ogrin. Though its territory rivals that of the three human kingdoms combined, its population is far more dispersed, with countless villages and market towns governing themselves through local councils. Rather than a kingdom, the Moot is a civilisation united by shared traditions, celebration and faith, where every community chooses its own path while remaining part of something greater._

_Each year the halflings gather for the Annual Moot, a week-long festival of trade, competitions, music, storytelling and feasting that concludes with representatives meeting to discuss matters affecting all halflings. Decisions are rarely unanimous, but the gathering itself serves as a reminder that despite their many differences, they remain one people. While the Moot has enjoyed centuries of peace and prosperity, the death of the King of [[Stolan]] and the brutal wars that followed have left many halflings questioning whether their neighbours can still be trusted and whether their way of life can endure in an increasingly dangerous world._

## History

- **Founding:**
    - Halfling communities spread naturally across the fertile heartlands of Ogrin, establishing independent settlements rather than a unified kingdom.
    - Over generations neighbouring communities began gathering each year to settle disputes, celebrate together and strengthen friendships, eventually becoming the Annual Moot.
- **Major Historical Events:**
    - The teachings of [[Halfling Pantheon|The Hearthwyn]] became the foundation of halfling culture.
    - Trade with [[Khazrim]] flourished, creating centuries of mutual respect between dwarves and halflings.
    - The King of [[Stolan]] earned widespread admiration among the Moot through years of diplomacy and genuine friendship with the halfling people.
- **Current Situation:**
    - The wars between the human kingdoms have left many halflings increasingly wary of human rulers.
    - Debate continues over whether the Moot should adopt stronger leadership as the world grows more dangerous.
    - Despite growing concerns, most settlements remain committed to local independence.

## Government & Military

- **Political Structure:** (Confederation)
    1. Independent Settlements
    2. Village Councils and Elders
    3. Representatives of the Annual Moot
- **Military Overview:**
    - No standing army.
    - Local militias formed when required.
    - Every settlement is responsible for its own defence.
    - Skilled hunters, rangers and volunteers answer the call during times of crisis.
    - Though lacking organisation, the combined population of the Moot could rival the greatest armies of Ogrin if ever united.
- **Laws or Unique Customs:**
    - Every settlement governs itself.
    - The decisions of the Moot carry influence rather than legal authority.
    - Hospitality is considered sacred.
    - Violence during the Annual Moot is one of the gravest taboos in halfling society.

## Culture

- **Values:**
    - Community
    - Celebration
    - Courage
    - Hospitality
    - Independence
- **Traditions/Festivals:**
    - The Annual Moot.
    - Harvest Celebrations.
    - Hearthfeasts.
    - Days of Remembrance.
- **Everyday Life:**
    - Though united by the teachings of [[Halfling Pantheon|The Hearthwyn]], halfling culture varies greatly across the Moot. Every community embraces life in its own way while sharing the belief that a meaningful life is one lived with purpose, kindness and courage.
    - The **Plainsfolk** cultivate the great farmlands and form the largest communities throughout the Moot.
    - The **Stonefolk** inhabit the foothills of the Worldspine Mountains, maintaining close trade with [[Khazrim]] and adopting many practical traditions from their dwarven neighbours.
    - The **Greenfolk** dwell within the ancient forests, where generations of druids have fostered a deep respect for the natural world.
    - The **Quietfolk** live in lands where the boundary between the Material and Ethereal Planes feels unusually close. More reserved than other halflings, they preserve traditions surrounding remembrance, ancestry and the passage of souls, serving as respected guides during times of mourning while celebrating life in quieter, more reflective ways.
    - Despite their differences, all halflings gather each year at the Annual Moot, where old friendships are renewed, new stories are shared and communities celebrate another year of life together.
